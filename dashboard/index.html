Overview — a sequential financial decision problem

An agent observes a market state each day and chooses a target exposure state: CASH (no exposure to the traded asset) or INVESTED (full exposure). Only a change of state is a transaction: CASH → INVESTED is a BUY, INVESTED → CASH is a SELL. Staying INVESTED is not a repeated BUY. Each purchase or sale has a modelled 0.10% trading fee plus a 0.05% allowance for slippage, totaling 0.15% of the traded value. Slippage is the difference between the reference price and the execution price; the allowance models that difference. These assumptions are not guaranteed real-world costs. Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) learns the policy on a TRAIN window; a VALIDATION window fixed every design choice; a prospectively untouched TEST window was evaluated exactly once.

R1
financial portfolio log return per step after transaction costs.
R2
R1 minus a turnover penalty (0.0005 per transaction leg) minus an incremental-drawdown penalty (0.10 × increase in drawdown). Reward shaping only; the financial accounting is identical in all four experiments.

Research questions

  1. RQ1 Can an RL agent learn a risk-aware policy from historical market observations? (E4 vs baselines)
  2. RQ2 Does an engineered state representation change out-of-sample behaviour relative to a minimal representation? (E2 vs E1, E4 vs E3)
  3. RQ3 Do explicit turnover and drawdown penalties produce more stable behaviour than a purely financial reward? (E3 vs E1, E4 vs E2)
